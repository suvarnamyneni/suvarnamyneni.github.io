Problem

Recipes, ingredients, and delivery were three errands.

Online grocery shopping was rising in India. The need the case study names, from research and personal experience, is one platform: curated recipes, the ingredients for them, and same-day delivery.

A common sentiment in the survey: when cooking an elaborate dish, people were often missing one or two ingredients, and had to make another trip.

Competitive research

Fresh already had the 2-hour delivery.

Among the grocery apps examined, Amazon Fresh was the only one with a 2-hour delivery service. BigBasket let people shop recipe ingredients, and fell short on timely delivery and the experience around it. That combination is why the case study treats Fresh as the place to build the feature.

Survey

48 people, 18 to 55.

The survey included 48 people, ages 18 to 55: students, employees and homemakers who cook and buy groceries for themselves. Questions covered offline grocery shopping, features they would add, whether recipes are easy to find online, how often they lack an ingredient, and how likely they were to use a feature that did this with same-day delivery.

95.8% of participants preferred shopping recipe ingredients with 2-hour delivery.

Personas and flow

Three personas, then one path through Fresh.

Three personas widened the scenarios the feature had to cover, between offline grocery shopping and the end user. The flow starts on the Amazon Fresh homepage, opens Shop by Recipe, then search, filters or featured recipes. A recipe page holds ingredients, directions and notes from expert chefs. People can change quantities, add or remove items, look at related recipes, add ingredients to the cart, and check out.

Shop by Recipe

Veg or non-veg, time, chef, and a direct buy.

Recipes are curated by top chefs for an Indian audience. The landing page has a veg/non-veg toggle, filters, time-duration indicators, the chef’s name, user ratings, and a way to buy ingredients without opening the recipe. “See how it works” in the header walks through the feature in pictures and text.

Recipe page

Change the servings, and the quantities follow.

The page opens with the title and a carousel. People can share the recipe or add it to a wishlist. A serving-size menu updates the quantities. Difficulty runs from Easy to Hard, and the time grows with it. Ingredients can go straight to the cart, or the shopper can skip to buying them. Directions are step by step. Verified users can add images, reviews and a rating.

Search returns recipes across categories. Filters cover category, cuisine, difficulty and customer rating.

Cart

More than one recipe in the same list.

The cart lists ingredients from the recipe page, with quantities people can change or delete. A newer behaviour shops ingredients for multiple recipes at once. The case study’s example shows two recipes in the list.
