The challenge

To answer a simple question, a new agent first had to know which tool to open.

Where is my revenue share? Who is my sponsor? Which transaction needs me? Each question lived in its own system, and each system looked and behaved differently. In NPS feedback, this struggle to find information was the number one concern among new agents.

How might we give new agents one place to ask, and one place to trust?

The hub problem

The work was to earn trust in one hub.

My eXp is the central place for an agent's organization, revenue share, team and mentorship. Agents still moved between many apps. The numbers they relied on had to be trustworthy and on time. The team shipped on a roughly biweekly rhythm, where one bad data release could take the hub down.

A cleaner coat of paint would have left that problem in place. The hub had to be the place agents trusted enough to stop opening the other tools.

How an answer moves

Mira sits on top of the systems agents already use.

  1. 01

    Start the conversation

    On login, Mira opens with a “Good Morning” prompt. On each page it offers starter questions about that page.

  2. 02

    Ground the answer

    The answer comes from the agent's own productivity in My eXp, transactions in SkySlope, and help articles in the eXpert Care knowledge base.

  3. 03

    Answer, then hand off

    Plain language, a small chart where numbers help, and a next step: a deep link into My eXp, a pin to Home, or a route to eXpert Care when Mira cannot help.

Reliability audit

The outage log is why trust came first.

Between early March and early April 2025 the hub log records 6 severity-1 incidents and 1 severity-2, across production and lower environments. One severity-1 was in production, after a data-services release. The rest were in lower environments: test environments that blocked demos, a login misroute, and transaction data that arrived late.

That is the context for putting Reliability & Trust first on the roadmap. Planned measures tracked hotfixes, active users, test coverage, support tickets and My eXp aNPS. No values were recorded. They are listed again under Outcomes, with the limits that were written down.

Early decisions

Mira starts the conversation, and stays inside what the data can support.

These are the MVP decisions from the team's key decisions. Where the notes record a trade-off, it is in the third column. The React front end is part of the same MVP list, and the notes do not record a trade-off for it.

MVP decisions and the trade-off recorded for each
Decision Why Trade-off
Ground Mira in the agent's own My eXp data The chat and the hub have to agree. Personal, accurate answers were what early adopters rated positively. Mira can answer what the data supports, so more questions hit a fallback early. Accuracy depends on the data pipeline, and the outage log showed that pipeline was fragile.
External apps open in a new tab Mira guides the agent to the right place. Rebuilding SkySlope and the other tools inside chat would have added scope and risk. Agents still change context for some tasks. The promise is one place to ask.
“Good Morning” prompts on login Proactive prompts are in the MVP. New agents do not know what to ask, and an empty box is a dead end. Page-aware starter chips do the same job once they are inside a screen. Prompts can feel noisy, and each page needs its own set.
Luna knowledge base deferred Kept out of the MVP to protect scope. Mira draws on the eXpert Care knowledge base, so there is one knowledge source to keep accurate. Some questions Luna might have answered fall back, or go to a person.

Working principles

Five rules for an assistant agents can trust.

The roadmap put Reliability & Trust first. The charter asked for trustworthy, timely information. I turned that into five rules I could design against.

  1. 01

    Their data, and general advice stays out

    Answers come from the agent's own My eXp, SkySlope and eXpert Care data, so the chat and the hub stay in agreement.

  2. 02

    Show where the answer lives

    Answers link into the My eXp screen they came from. In eXp Pulse, each pinned insight shows the question behind it and when it was last refreshed.

  3. 03

    Point, and open the tool in a new tab

    Mira sends the agent to the right system. It does not rebuild every tool inside the chat window.

  4. 04

    “I don't know” is a designed moment

    When Mira cannot answer, it says what it can do, or it hands the agent to a person. A dead-end apology is the failure the thumbs later clustered on.

  5. 05

    Every answer can be rated

    Thumbs up and thumbs down in the chat give the team a steady signal for which answers to fix first.

Six roadmap themes, reliability first

  • Reliability & Trust Faster data and load times, data accuracy, security and privacy, and test automation across unit, regression and performance.
  • Agent self-service Personalization such as custom dashboards, self-serve tasks staff currently do, and more tool integrations.
  • Global expansion Language support, region-based insights, Canada and other markets.
  • Mandatory enhancements Executive requests, EXPCON and compliance work.
  • Agent production “Move the needle”: visibility for team leads, organization leads and mentors.
  • Platform consolidation Fewer third-party tools and licenses, with more of that work built into My eXp.

Design

Dark navy reporting gave way to one light system.

I compared the earlier hub screens, Home, revenue share, the organization tree and ICON, with the latest prototype. Four shifts explain the move.

  1. 01

    Motivation over reporting

    Home became Mission Control. Agents set goals, see progress and year-over-year chips, and get Weekly Quests capped at three so the dashboard can close.

  2. 02

    Less to parse at once

    Tinted off-white cards, soft shadows, category-tinted icon chips, 10% tint pills, and heavy numerals. In the prototype those numerals are set in Manrope at weight 800.

  3. 03

    A Home that can change shape

    Agents drag, hide and reset widgets. Upline Partners and Getting Started moved from fixed blocks to optional widgets. The influencer-status banner is off the default Home.

  4. 04

    Whole areas that were missing

    Team roster and reconciliation, business transactions and referrals, Documents, mentor flows, Contact eXpert Care and Notifications.

Mira

A layer on every page, with the first question already written.

A separate chat destination would have been one more place to learn. The panel opens beside the data the agent is already looking at. Putting it on every page costs a prompt set per page, and it keeps the answer next to the record.

  • Chat panel Opens beside the page with prompts such as GCI trends, listing velocity and active pipeline.
  • eXp Pulse Pinned daily and weekly insights, a performance overview and quick actions.
  • History Past conversations, searchable and grouped by topic. The prototype's History screen is titled “Home”, which is a labeling bug, so that capture stays out of the hero.

Conversation design

New agents do not know what to ask, so Mira asks first.

The opening line and the starter prompts change with the page. Every answer suggests where to go next.

  • Page-aware openers Transactions on Transactions, cap and award tiers on ICON, earnings and the sponsor tree on RevShare.
  • Starter chips Three one-tap prompts per page, so the first question is a tap.
  • Follow-ups An answer with a chart offers two next questions, for example “How does this compare to my team?”
  • Keep what is useful An agent can pin an insight to Home. Mira confirms it and offers “View on Dashboard”. History is searchable, and voice mode covers hands-busy moments.

Testing

+60 from five people, and a pattern in 27 thumbs.

June 2025 early-adopter NPS was +60, from 5 valid responses: 3 promoters, 2 detractors, 0 passives. With five people, one response moves the score by 20 points. I treated it as a direction, and I kept the sample size next to the number.

3 promoters 2 detractors

0 passives. Promoters, scores of 10, often cited internal alignment and their commitment as recruiters. Detractors raised missing features and an inconsistent experience. People asked for transparency in agent support, more than once, and for UI improvements such as resource links and visual clarity.

27 thumbs

8 up, 19 down.

Of 27 ratings, 8 were positive, about 30%, and 19 were negative, about 70%. Too few for a score. Enough to show a pattern.

8 up 19 down

Negatives clustered on generic replies

Fallback failures: “having trouble”, “rephrasing”, “connect”. Those were mostly generic fallback messages.

Apology loop: “sorry”, “expert”, “care”. A default apology when Mira had no answer.

Technical errors: standard error responses that did not resolve the question.

Positives were specific and personal

Performance summaries and metrics queries landed well. So did answers that came back with the agent's own, accurate data.

The feedback rejected the moments Mira had no answer. The idea of the assistant held. That set the go-live order.

How I read the 27

  1. 01

    Count honestly

    27 ratings: 8 up, 19 down.

  2. 02

    Group by what Mira said

    The same words kept coming back in the negative answers: “having trouble”, “rephrasing”, “connect”, “sorry”, “expert care”.

  3. 03

    Name the failure

    Three kinds: generic fallbacks, a default apology loop, and technical error messages. The idea of Mira was not the thing that failed.

  4. 04

    Turn it into an order

    The positives were specific and personal. Go-live order: data accuracy, response quality, session context, and keeping feedback coming in.

Going live

Ship less, and get the answers right.

Data accuracy. Numbers must match My eXp, SkySlope and the knowledge base.

Response quality. Fewer generic fallbacks, more direct answers.

Session context. Mira remembers what you asked earlier in the session.

Feedback. Keep the in-chat thumbs coming in, so the next fixes have a signal.

What came out of the first release, and why
Decision Why What we gave up
Hide trending topics The thumbs said accuracy was the problem, ahead of more prompts. 4 of 5 early adopters had rated trending prompts very important. With n = 5, we went against a stated preference.
Hide the explore pages Performance, Transactions and Marketing explore pages were more surface on which to be wrong. The demo's Experts menu story is shorter at launch.
First access for Mastermind agents A smaller, engaged group, with feedback still coming in before a wider launch. It went out as “first access”. Slower reach. Early feedback skews toward experienced agents, away from the new agents the work was aimed at.

Reflection

An assistant is judged by its worst answer.

  1. 01

    Get “I don't know” right

    Most of the negative ratings, about 70% of 27, came from fallbacks. Designing that moment is part of the product.

  2. 02

    Small samples are signals

    +60 from 5 people told us where to look. The sample size belongs next to the number, every time.

  3. 03

    Cutting scope is a design decision

    Hiding trending topics and the explore pages let us launch something accurate, to Mastermind agents first.

  4. 04

    Open the other tool in a new tab

    That choice kept Mira as a front door. The agent still has a system to finish the job in.

  5. 05

    Prototype the edge cases

    Toggles for Canada, capped and empty states put those real situations in the review.

What I would do next with AI

My recommendations. The launch shape above is the shipped scope in this record.

  1. 01

    Ask why on a thumbs-down

    One-tap reasons, such as a wrong number, an answer that missed the question, or a reply that was too generic, so each “no” says what to fix.

  2. 02

    Track the fallback rate

    How often Mira says it does not know, and how often that ends with a person helping, alongside the overall thumbs-up rate.

  3. 03

    A standing test set

    Real agent questions with known answers from the three sources, run before each release, next to the UAT gate.

  4. 04

    Show the source on every answer

    A small “from My eXp” or “from SkySlope” label with a timestamp, so the agent can trust the number and stay in the chat.
