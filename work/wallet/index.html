Problem

People run out of balance mid-shop, and nobody tracks it all.

Running out of balance while shopping is common, and it usually comes down to habit. Some people just do not watch their balances and expenses. That does not mean they should spend less. The real trouble is tracking balances and expenses across many platforms.

So the brief I set myself: a way to record balances and expenses that improves people's transactional habits.

The idea

Record expenses and track balances. Nothing more.

  • Bill scanner Expenses are recorded by scanning the bill, using optical character recognition.
  • Connected accounts People connect every fintech account they have, so balances live in one place.
  • Spend prediction The app predicts total spending from the expense records.

Discover

Three kinds of people, one set of questions.

Before writing interview questions I split the audience into three segments:

Never record

People who never record their expenses.

Record by hand

People who record them without a dedicated app: a notebook, digital notes, or Excel.

Use an app

People who regularly use a financial recording app.

The interviews covered monthly spending, cash or cashless and why, which e-wallets people use, whether they keep bills, what happens when they run out of money while shopping, and whether they would want an app that tracks every balance or limits spending by category.

Define

Fikri, the Lazy Spender.

The persona came out of the interviews: a 20-year-old student living overseas, with money spread across several fintech platforms. He does not know how much he has spent in a month, or how much he has left, and he is too lazy to log expenses one at a time.

  • Goals Easy financial records, every fintech account in one app, and a clear view of the month's expenses.
  • Frustrations Recording expenses feels like a chore, and he never really knows his balance.

Empathy map

"I don't really know how much money I have left in total."

The pains were time, laziness, and not being used to taking notes. The gains were a fast way to record, easy tracking, and seeing total spend with some analysis behind it. That pair shaped everything after: recording had to take almost no effort.

Ideate

One path from a new note to the report.

The user flow runs from register and login to home, connecting accounts, and a new note. A note is either a scanned bill or a manual entry. From there people check history or the monthly and quarterly report, then start a new note.

Design

Crazy 8s on paper, then wireframes.

I sketched with Crazy 8s, the Design Sprint exercise of eight distinct ideas in eight minutes. The low-fidelity wireframes came straight from those sketches.

Design system

Calm indigo, and SF Pro Text.

A primary range of soft violets into deep indigo, a secondary set for status, SF Pro Text throughout, and a ten-step text hierarchy from 22px display down to 9px.

Final screens

Onboarding, then straight to the balance.

Three onboarding cards (scan, organise, get insights), then create an account or log in, with Google as an option.

Home and bill scanner

Photograph the bill. It becomes a note.

Home shows balances across every account, total spend and the latest expense. To add an expense, people take or pick a photo of the bill. The items come through as a digital note, and they pick a category and payment method before finishing.

Manual add

No bill? A few steps by hand.

The manual note has two tabs. Transaction takes a category, item, price, quantity and payment method. Transfer records money moved from one account to another. Finish becomes active once the note is filled in.

History, report and profile

Every note in one list, and next month's estimate.

History splits transactions from transfers and filters by category. Report shows a monthly breakdown by category and a quarterly view, with a prediction of this month's spend based on daily expenses. Profile holds categories, PIN, language, currency and dark mode.
